# Stonevow

**The Rime Wards, Book One**
*Iona Rask*

---

**The wards hold because someone pays for them. She never meant to fall in love with the payment.**

I have set forty-one wards. I buried the man who taught me and the woman who kept him warm, and in six years of holding this line alone, I have never once asked for a hearth of my own.

That is the whole of the arrangement: a wardwright to shape the stone, a hearth to feed it, and a name read aloud at the binding so the village knows, in advance, precisely whose funeral to expect.

The rite reads Aldric Marrow's name.

He volunteered — traded his own body for his sister's exemption, sight unseen, before he ever knew mine. He treats the cold like a rumour and me like a person, not an office the crown assigned him to. And through the bond neither of us asked for, I can feel exactly how much of him the stone is taking, one ward at a time.

Three months of winter. Forty miles of failing cairns. A corrupted rite that has always spent one hearth alone and called it mercy. And somewhere underneath the frost, a mutual form nobody has dared attempt in generations — one that shares the cost instead of hiding it, if either of us has the nerve to ask for it properly, in front of witnesses, before the Rime finishes what six years of careful, solitary sacrifice couldn't stop.

*Stonevow* is a slow-burn, dual-POV Romantasy about a rite that was never supposed to be survivable alone, and the two people who rewrite it in front of the whole line.

---

A wardwright shapes the stone. A hearth feeds it. The line holds until one of them runs out. Three months of winter, forty miles of failing cairns, and a bond that makes every cost she takes his as well — until she decides that isn't the arrangement she's willing to keep.
