## Chapter Eight — Terms

The leak showed up in the timestamps, because everything showed up
somewhere.

Elena ran the register audit alone on a dead Monday, the way she ran
one every quarter, and the tills were perfect to the dollar — but the
system log was not perfect to the minute. Four times in three weeks,
the office terminal had been woken after close. Two, three minutes at
a time. Nothing opened but the scheduling screen and the reservation
book. Someone was reconstructing her day after her day was over: when
she'd come in, when she'd left, who had booked the private room.

She sat back and let the cold walk up her arms.

Not theft — thieves opened the money screens. Surveillance. She built
the suspect column the way she built any column: opportunity, access,
hours. And then she did a thing she had never once done in eleven
years of ruthless arithmetic. She reached the line where the column
wanted a name whose knife roll lived eight feet from that terminal,
and she refused the entry.

*Nico has a man on the night porter crew,* she wrote instead, in her
head, in ink. *Nico bought a copy of the alley key years ago. Nico,
Nico, Nico.* Plausible. Consistent with the car counting her lights.
She audited everyone in the building and priced Lucia at zero, because
some accounts you do not open, because if that account was open then
nothing in her life had ever been solvent.

The false belief filed itself with the smooth click of a drawer
closing. She never heard it.

What she did instead — and she caught the size of it even as she did
it — was pull a blank card from the drawer and write, in the narrow
script, a thought she'd been carrying for a week: *If I fall, the book
needs a second custodian. Someone the family can't buy and can't
scare.*

The column where that name went had a name in it already. It wore a
coat like procedure. She looked at what her own handwriting had done,
and put the card in the safe with the ledger, on top, like a codicil.

She picked the diner on Ohio Street because it was nobody's — vinyl
booths, bus fumes, coffee like punishment, a place the family would
never eat and the Bureau would never expense. Neutral ground has a
smell. It smells like burnt orange peel and freedom.

Cole came in with snow on his shoulders, clocked the room in one
sweep, and sat across from her with the wariness of a man who had
been summoned by a source, which he had not been. He'd been summoned
by her. The distinction was the meeting.

"I have terms," Elena said.

He went very still. "You'll want counsel present—"

"I want a napkin present." She pulled one from the dispenser and laid
her pen on it. "Here's the offer. I give you the structure. Org
chart, money flow, who answers to whom, how the tribute moves, what
the vending route really is. Names and arrows. Enough to aim every
subpoena you'll ever need." She turned the pen a quarter-turn,
aligning it with the napkin's edge. "Not the ledger. Nothing in my
hand, nothing in my father's. And in exchange: Danny gets a
protective transfer this week — this week, Cole — and when the
building comes down, the fourteen people on that payroll who just
cook and carry plates walk out clean. A lane for the innocents. I
mark the lane, you keep it open."

"That's cooperation. The office will want it papered, proffered—"

"The office wanted me wired," she said, and watched the hit land.
"You stood in a room where they priced me like equipment, and you bid
procedure until they stopped. Don't audit how I know. So no — no
paper, no proffer, no wire. Terms between you and me, kept because we
keep them."

The waitress refilled their cups. Cole looked at the napkin for a
long time, and she watched the war run its whole course through his
jaw — the manual, the case, the dead witness, the SAC, and beneath
it all the thing that had been true since a doorway on Green Street.

"Off book," he said finally, quietly, like a diagnosis.

"You've been off book since one fifty-four in the morning,
Investigator. I'm just offering you double-entry."

He took out his own pen — of course he wouldn't use hers, there was a
section somewhere — and wrote on the napkin in block letters:
STRUCTURE, NOT SOURCE DOCUMENTS. TRANSFER D.M. LANE FOR NON-
PRINCIPALS. And under it, the date, because he was who he was.

"This isn't trust," she said, initialing it.

"No," he agreed. "It's terms."

"Good. Terms I can audit."

Their hands were ten inches apart on a formica table, closing the
first joint account either had opened in years, and the diner's
window fogged against the cold, and it occurred to Elena — with the
specific vertigo of a bookkeeper catching her own error years deep —
that the exit plan had quietly stopped being the plan. This was the
plan now. Alliance. Napkin law. The slow entangling of two people who
each knew exactly what entangling cost.

Midpoint, her father would have called it. The moment a long account
stops being two columns and becomes one.
